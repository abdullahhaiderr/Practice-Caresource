# Content review — PracticeCareSource

Last reviewed: November 7, 2026

## Editorial rule

Do not invent or imply business facts, credentials, performance results, customer outcomes, legal details, contact details, or supported specialties. If information has not been confirmed by the business, leave it unpublished or label it clearly as a placeholder in internal documentation.

## Confirmed public identity

- Brand: PracticeCareSource.
- Public contact email: `info@practicecaresource.com`.
- Core positioning: medical billing and revenue-cycle support for healthcare practices in the United States.
- Primary website CTA: “Request Free Consultation”.
- Canonical fallback while domain setup is unverified: `https://practice-caresource.vercel.app`.

## Do not publish without confirmation

- Registered legal entity name, including “PracticeCareSource LLC”.
- Phone number, mailing address, office address, and business hours.
- Exact supported medical specialties.
- Any exact percentage fee, pricing rate, or financial performance metric.
- Client names, logos, testimonials, case studies, star ratings, and review counts.
- Named staff, credentials, years of experience, awards, payer relationships, or certifications.
- Claims that the company is “HIPAA certified” or guarantees compliance.
- Guarantees about reimbursement, revenue increases, clean-claim rates, payer decisions, enrollment timelines, denial overturns, or audit outcomes.

## Services and specialties

The site describes medical billing and revenue-cycle work through its service pages. Review the exact service catalog with the business before launch and keep the published descriptions within the agreed scope.

Specialty pages and specialty-specific copy must remain unpublished from search indexing until the business confirms the specialties it supports. Do not infer supported specialties from generic examples or industry norms.

AI automation is a supporting capability only; do not position it as a separate primary service unless the business changes the approved scope.

## Contact form and PHI

The consultation form requests business contact details and includes a free-text message field. It warns visitors not to submit patient names, dates of birth, medical record numbers, claim documents, insurance card details, or other PHI.

Because the visitor controls free-text input, the website cannot technically guarantee that PHI will never be submitted. Avoid saying the form “prevents” or “blocks” PHI. Do not add file uploads or patient-data fields to the marketing site.

The API must not log inquiry contents. Email delivery should be enabled only with approved server-side configuration and a monitored recipient.

## Legal and compliance review

The Privacy Policy, Terms and Conditions, Cookie Policy, and HIPAA/security page are informational website content, not legal advice or a certification. Qualified counsel should review:

- Exact legal entity name and governing-law jurisdiction.
- Privacy disclosures against actual data collection, hosting, email, and retention practices.
- Liability, indemnification, venue, and dispute terms.
- State privacy law applicability.
- Business Associate Agreement workflow and actual safeguards before client PHI is exchanged.

Do not state that PracticeCareSource is HIPAA certified. Do not make blanket compliance guarantees.

## Editorial standards

- Use plain language and explain specialized billing terms.
- Describe services and process steps specifically, without generic “world-class” claims.
- State material limitations alongside capabilities.
- Avoid fear-based messaging and guaranteed outcomes.
- Keep blog content informational and clearly distinguish it from legal, coding, or compliance advice.
- Review dates, links, citations, and regulatory references before publishing updates.
